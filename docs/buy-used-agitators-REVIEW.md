### FAQ Tab: Specifications

**Q: What types of used agitators does IPP sell?**

IPP sells three main categories of agitators: glass-lined drive agitators designed for glass-lined reactors, standard drive agitators for open tanks and non-glass-lined vessels, and shaft/mixer agitators consisting of shafts with impellers and blades. Manufacturers include Pfaudler, Lightnin, DeDietrich, Philadelphia, Chemineer, Kestermann, Ekato, and others. **Q: What is the difference between a glass-lined drive and a standard drive agitator?**

Glass-lined drive agitators are designed specifically for glass-lined reactors. They feature borosilicate glass-coated wetted parts — including the stuffing box, bearing housing, and shaft sealing area — to prevent metal contamination in corrosive pharmaceutical and chemical processes. Standard drive agitators use bare metal construction (carbon steel, stainless steel, or Hastelloy) and are suited for open tanks, stainless steel reactors, and other non-glass-lined vessels where corrosion resistance comes from the shaft and impeller metallurgy rather than a glass lining. Glass-lined drives from Pfaudler and DeDietrich are the most commonly sourced replacement agitators in the process equipment market.

**Q: What power ranges are available for used agitators?**

IPP stocks agitators with power ratings from 0.2 kW (0.3 HP) for small pilot-scale reactors up to 100.7 kW (135 HP) for large production-scale mixing vessels. The most commonly stocked power ratings are in the 0.75 to 37.3 kW (1 to 50 HP) range, Transmission types include belt drive and direct coupled. Contact IPP with your reactor specifications and our team will identify matching agitators from current inventory.

**Q: How do I match an agitator to my reactor?** ⚠️

Matching an agitator to your reactor requires specifying the reactor manufacturer and model (e.g., Pfaudler 4RW, DeDietrich), vessel size and nozzle configuration, required power rating based on process viscosity and mixing intensity, shaft length and impeller type, and seal type (mechanical seal or stuffing box). For glass-lined reactors, the agitator drive must be specifically compatible with the reactor cover and nozzle geometry. Provide your reactor nameplate data and IPP will identify the correct replacement drive from inventory.

**Q: What industries rely most on agitator drives?**

Chemical, pharmaceutical, food, beverage, cosmetics, and water treatment industries all rely on agitator drives for consistent mixing. In chemical manufacturing, agitator drives power mixers for homogenization, emulsification, and reaction support. In pharmaceutical production, they drive sanitary mixers for sterile blending and suspension. In food and beverage processing, agitator drives mix sauces, beverages, slurries, and syrups. In water and wastewater treatment, they agitate tanks for chemical dosing, flocculation, and biological treatment. In cosmetic and personal care manufacturing, agitator drives blend creams, lotions, and emulsions.

**Q: How do agitator drives compare to complete mixer assemblies?**

Agitator drives power the mechanical mixing elements; they can be sold separately for integration into existing tanks or as part of full mixer systems. A complete mixer assembly includes the drive, shaft, impeller, seal, and mounting hardware. IPP stocks agitator drives across all three categories: glass-lined, standard drives, and shaft/mixer assemblies. Contact IPP to discuss what is available for your specific requirements.

**Q: What maintenance is required for agitator drives?**

Routine inspection of seals, bearings, and motor components, along with lubrication and alignment checks, ensures reliable performance and extended drive life. Belt-drive units require belt tension checks and periodic belt replacement. Direct-coupled units require coupling alignment verification.

### FAQ Tab: Comparisons

**Q: What is the difference between a re-glassed and a new glass-lined agitator drive?**

A re-glassed agitator drive uses the original OEM housing (typically Pfaudler or DeDietrich) with a new borosilicate glass lining applied. IPP offers re-glassing through its UGE (Universal Glasteel Equipment) division. A new glass-lined drive is manufactured from scratch by the OEM with a new housing and new glass lining. **Q: Pfaudler vs DeDietrich — which glass-lined drive should I choose?**

The choice between Pfaudler and DeDietrich glass-lined drives depends primarily on your reactor manufacturer. Pfaudler drives are designed for Pfaudler glass-lined reactors and feature the RW, TW, and DTW mounting configurations. DeDietrich drives are designed for DeDietrich reactors and use their own mounting geometry. Cross-compatibility between manufacturers may require adapter plates and is not always possible. Matching the agitator drive manufacturer to your reactor manufacturer is the standard approach for compatibility. Both manufacturers use borosilicate glass lining technology. Contact IPP to confirm compatibility for your specific reactor.

**Q: Belt drive vs direct coupled — which transmission type is better?**

Belt-drive and direct-coupled transmissions serve different applications. Belt-drive agitators (the majority of glass-lined drives in IPP stock) use a V-belt and pulley system between the motor and gearbox, providing speed flexibility and vibration isolation that protects the glass lining. Direct-coupled agitators connect the motor directly to the gearbox through a coupling, delivering higher mechanical efficiency and requiring less maintenance. Glass-lined reactor applications typically use belt-drive transmission because the belt absorbs vibration that could damage the glass lining. Standard drive applications in open tanks and stainless steel vessels more commonly use direct-coupled transmission for maximum power transfer.
