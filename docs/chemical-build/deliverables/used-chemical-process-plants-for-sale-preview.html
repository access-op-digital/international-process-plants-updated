Buy used chemical plants and equipment from International Process Plants (IPP)

International Process Plants (IPP) sells used chemical process plants and equipment to chemical, petrochemical, fertilizer, polymer, agrochemical and specialty chemical producers worldwide. IPP stocks complete plants, process lines and individual reactors, heat exchangers, centrifuges, dryers, filters, distillation columns and tanks from Pfaudler, De Dietrich, Alfa Laval, Krauss Maffei, Westfalia and other OEM manufacturers. Purchasing used from IPP can save up to 50% of capital and 90% of lead time versus buying new. IPP has supplied process equipment worldwide since 1980.

International Process Plants (IPP) is the world’s largest seller of used process plants and equipment, with 15,000+ pieces of inventory and nearly five decades of experience. IPP owns 20 complete process plant sites. Materials of construction include glass-lined, stainless steel 316, 316L, 304 and 321, Hastelloy C-22 and C-276, titanium, graphite and polypropylene. Capacities range from 2 L (0.5 gal) reactors to 565,000 L (149,258 gal) tanks. Reactor pressure ratings reach 358.7 bar (5,203 psi).

Condition options include used, unused, refurbished, re-glassed and new. IPP offers re-glassed glass-lined equipment through Universal Glasteel Equipment (UGE) and new stainless steel equipment through Gale Process Solutions (GPS). IPP’s reactor inventory includes ASME, National Board, CRN, CE and PED coded vessels.

IPP buys shutdown chemical plants, idle process lines and surplus equipment from plant owners and asset managers. IPP values each asset at the seller’s site. IPP handles decommissioning, dismantling and removal. Plant owners start a sale through the sell-a-plant or sell-equipment form. Buyers request specifications, inspections and quotations from IPP’s team.

How buying from IPP works

Bring your engineering requirements. IPP helps you establish the asset and service scope.

  1. Share your requirements

    Send the process, required capacity, materials of construction, operating conditions, destination and target timing. Include any stock numbers you have selected.

  2. Review specifications and inspect

    Request available photographs, drawings and equipment records. Arrange a video or on-site inspection with the IPP team.

  3. Agree the purchase scope

    Confirm the equipment included, commercial terms and services required. Ask about financing, which IPP advertises for its plants and equipment.

  4. Plan dismantling and relocation

    Coordinate teardown, loading and relocation requirements for the selected plant or system. Establish responsibilities and schedules before removal begins.

  5. Arrange shipping and start-up support

    IPP offers international shipping options, or buyers can arrange their own shipping. Discuss destination-site installation and start-up support as part of the project scope.

Sell chemical plants and surplus equipment to IPP

IPP buys complete chemical plants, process lines and individual surplus equipment from shutdown, idled and restructured facilities. Its team handles intact, mothballed and partially dismantled plant sites.

IPP reports that Fortune 100 and Fortune 500 companies typically represent 95% of its sellers. Selling used chemical equipment to IPP replaces piecemeal disposal and ends the seller’s storage and maintenance costs.

Sell a plant ↗Sell equipment ↗

  1. Send your asset information

    Send the asset list, photographs, plant location, available technical documents and removal deadline. IPP’s forms to sell a plant or sell equipment collect each item.

  2. IPP values the assets

    IPP reviews the information and inspects the plant site where needed. IPP then values the assets for an outright purchase or for marketing to its global buyer network.

  3. Agree the sale structure

    IPP buys equipment as-is, where-is, as a principal buyer rather than a broker. A complete plant sale can include the land, buildings, equipment and intellectual property.

  4. Decommissioning, dismantling and removal

    IPP coordinates plant decommissioning, dismantling, packaging, freight and export. IPP’s team works with the owner’s EHS team on permit closure, emissions compliance and utility shutoffs.

Used chemical process plant FAQs

IPP answers questions from buyers of second-hand chemical equipment and from sellers of used chemical machinery. Discuss a specific stock number, plant package or surplus asset with IPP.

Ask an expert ↗
Does IPP offer full chemical plants for sale?

Yes. In addition to individual equipment, we sell complete chemical process plants, including teardown and relocation services.

Can I inspect the equipment before buying?

Yes. IPP offers on-site and video inspections for most equipment. Contact us to schedule one.

Where does IPP source its chemical equipment?

From multinational chemical producers, toll manufacturers and decommissioned or restructured facilities worldwide.

What is chemical process plant equipment?

Chemical process plant equipment is the set of reactors, heat exchangers, centrifuges, dryers, filters, columns and tanks that converts raw materials into chemical products. Read IPP’s chemical process plant equipment guide.

How do I get a price for a chemical plant or equipment?

IPP quotes each plant and unit individually. Contact IPP with the stock number or your process requirements. The quotation defines the assets included and any dismantling, loading, shipping or start-up services.

Can IPP help with international shipping?

Yes. IPP coordinates packaging, crating, freight and delivery, or buyers can arrange their own shipping. Confirm the asset location, loading requirements and destination when you request a quotation.

What documentation is available for a used plant?

Documentation varies by asset. IPP shares the equipment lists, drawings, process descriptions and inspection records available for each plant or stock number.

Is financing available?

Yes. IPP advertises financing for its plants and equipment. Contact IPP to discuss terms for your purchase.

Does IPP sell new chemical process equipment?

Yes. Gale Process Solutions (GPS), an IPP group company, fabricates new stainless steel process equipment with 12 to 16 week average delivery. Universal Glasteel Equipment (UGE) supplies new and re-glassed glass-lined equipment.

Does IPP buy shutdown chemical plants and surplus equipment?

Yes. IPP buys complete plants, process lines and individual equipment from shutdown, idled and restructured sites. Start with the sell a plant or sell equipment form.

What information should I provide when selling?

Provide an asset list, photographs, the plant location, available technical documents and your removal deadline. IPP’s team reviews the information and confirms the next steps.

Does IPP buy the land and buildings with a chemical plant?

Yes. IPP purchases complete plant sites, including the land, buildings, equipment and intellectual property. IPP also buys individual process units and equipment systems within a site. Read how selling to IPP works.

Does IPP assume environmental obligations at a plant site?

Yes, in some acquisitions. IPP buys sites with known contamination and assumes site cleanup and regulatory compliance obligations as the buyer. IPP’s remediation partners work with local environmental authorities on each site. Discuss a site exit with IPP.

What happens to a chemical plant site after a sale?

The purchase agreement defines the outcome. A sale can cover equipment for removal or the entire site, and IPP’s published projects include equipment relocation and site repurposing.
