How buying from IPP works

Bring your engineering requirements. IPP helps you establish the asset and service scope.

  1. Share your requirements:

    Send the process, required capacity, materials of construction, operating conditions, destination and target timing. Include any stock numbers you have selected.

  2. Review specifications and inspect:

    Request available photographs, drawings and equipment records. Arrange a video or on-site inspection with the IPP team.

  3. Agree the purchase scope:

    Confirm the equipment included, commercial terms and services required. Ask about financing, which IPP advertises for its plants and equipment.

  4. Plan dismantling and relocation:

    Coordinate teardown, loading and relocation requirements for the selected plant or system. Establish responsibilities and schedules before removal begins.

  5. Arrange shipping and start-up support:

    IPP offers international shipping options, or buyers can arrange their own shipping. Discuss destination-site installation and start-up support as part of the project scope.

Plant owners, asset managers and corporate teams start a sale by sending their asset information to IPP. Our forms to sell a plant or sell equipment collect each item for a complete site or a single surplus unit.

Information that starts a sale includes:

  • The asset list for the plant, line or unit
  • Photographs of the equipment and site
  • The plant location
  • Available technical documents
  • The removal deadline

Our team reviews the information and confirms the next steps. Sellers send the details through the sell-a-plant or sell-equipment form.

IPP values the assets

We review the information and inspect the plant site where needed. IPP then values the assets for an outright purchase or for marketing to its global buyer network.

Our valuation covers:

  • Complete plant sites with land, buildings, equipment and intellectual property
  • Process units and equipment systems within a site
  • Specialty alloy reactors and custom drying and distillation systems
  • Equipment too large, too specialized or too site-bound for traditional resellers
  • Intact, mothballed and partially dismantled plant sites

IPP then proposes a purchase or a marketing arrangement for the assets. The proposal reflects the asset type, condition, documentation and removal scope.

Agree the sale structure

IPP buys equipment as-is, where-is, as a principal buyer rather than a broker. We offer flexible purchase structures for a warehouse clearance, a plant closure or a portfolio sale.

Sale structures we offer include:

  • Outright purchase of equipment as-is, where-is
  • Complete plant sales including land, buildings, equipment and intellectual property
  • Marketing to IPP’s global buyer network
  • Site acquisitions where IPP assumes cleanup and compliance obligations
  • Portfolio sales covering more than one site

The purchase agreement defines the assets included, the outcome for the site and the removal schedule. Our team confirms each term before removal begins.

Decommissioning, dismantling and removal

IPP coordinates plant decommissioning, dismantling, packaging, freight and export. Our team works with the owner’s EHS team on permit closure, emissions compliance and utility shutoffs.

Our decommissioning and removal work covers:

  • Permit closure and emissions compliance with the owner’s EHS team
  • Utility shutoffs
  • Dismantling and removal of process equipment
  • Packaging, freight and export
  • Site preparation for return to the owner or transfer to a new operator

IPP’s decommissioning work carries ISN RAVS Plus verification. Our team plans dismantling around the seller’s removal deadline.

Chemical plant projects and industry recognition

Our chemical plant projects cover both sides of the market. Producers sell plants and sites through IPP, and buyers redeploy those assets in new production.

BASF · May 2024

Marketing major chemical plants

IPP and BASF agreed in May 2024 to market the ammonia, methanol and melamine plants at BASF’s Verbund site in Ludwigshafen, Germany. The integrated assets produce 380,000 metric tons of ammonia, 165,000 metric tons of methanol and 51,000 metric tons of melamine a year. We offer the plants for relocation and sale to buyers seeking lower capex and faster project execution.

Novartis · January 2024

Repurposing a pharmaceutical site

Humber Industrials, an IPP affiliate, agreed in January 2024 to purchase Novartis UK’s 170-acre API manufacturing site and assets in Grimsby. The site holds 90 reaction suites with 900 m³ (237,755 gal) of total capacity, 17 centrifuges, 26 dryer systems and 10 solvent recovery systems. Our Grimsby redevelopment project transitions the shuttered site into a viable future asset.

API equipment acquisition

Moving assets into their next use

IPP bought a U.S. API plant from a CDMO headquartered in Europe and dismantled it on an expedited schedule to meet the seller’s timeline. We moved 18 reactors, 20 tanks, four filter dryers, centrifuges, vacuum dryers and heat exchangers in Hastelloy, glass-lined steel, stainless steel and Karbate to our Eastover, South Carolina facility. The emptied building now serves as a warehouse.

Decommissioning · ISN RAVS Plus

Decommissioning and site transfer

IPP holds ISN RAVS Plus verification for plant decommissioning. We have completed full-scale decommissions in Ohio, Alabama, Louisiana, the United Kingdom and Germany. Our team helped transfer a shuttered biobased chemicals plant in Minnesota to Nuol Green Chemistry, a deal SOCMA highlighted.

Chemical Process Plants Resources

Used chemical process plants and equipment from IPP

International Process Plants (IPP) buys and sells used process plants and equipment from its headquarters at 410 Princeton Hightstown Road, Princeton Junction, New Jersey.

We operate offices in 15 countries: Brazil, Canada, China, the Czech Republic, France, Germany, India, Italy, Mexico, Pakistan, Portugal, Romania, Turkey, the United Kingdom and the United States. IPP has served 160,000+ customers worldwide.

Our chemical inventory covers complete plants, process lines and individual equipment such as reactors, heat exchangers, centrifuges, dryers, filters, distillation columns and tanks. IPP sources these assets from multinational chemical producers, toll manufacturers and decommissioned or restructured facilities.

The IPP group includes Gale Process Solutions (GPS), which fabricates new stainless steel process equipment, and Universal Glasteel Equipment (UGE), which supplies and re-glasses glass-lined equipment.

IPP affiliates acquired the GEO Specialty Chemicals site in Allentown, Pennsylvania, and agreed to acquire Novartis UK’s Grimsby API manufacturing site. In May 2024, IPP announced an agreement with BASF to market ammonia, methanol and melamine plants in Ludwigshafen, Germany.

Ross Gale, IPP’s vice president, wrote “Cost Engineering with Previously Owned Process Equipment” for Chemical Engineering in December 2024.

Used chemical process plant FAQs

We answer questions from buyers of second-hand chemical equipment and from sellers of used chemical machinery. Discuss a specific stock number, plant package or surplus asset with IPP.

Ask an expert

Does IPP offer full chemical plants for sale?

Yes. In addition to individual equipment, we sell complete chemical process plants, including teardown and relocation services.

Can I inspect the equipment before buying?

Yes. IPP offers on-site and video inspections for most equipment. Contact us to schedule one.

Where does IPP source its chemical equipment?

From multinational chemical producers, toll manufacturers and decommissioned or restructured facilities worldwide.

What is chemical process plant equipment?

Chemical process plant equipment is the set of reactors, heat exchangers, centrifuges, dryers, filters, columns and tanks that converts raw materials into chemical products. Read IPP’s chemical process plant equipment guide.

How do I get a price for a chemical plant or equipment?

We quote each plant and unit individually. Contact IPP with the stock number or your process requirements. The quotation defines the assets included and any dismantling, loading, shipping or start-up services.

Can IPP help with international shipping?

Yes. We coordinate packaging, crating, freight and delivery, or buyers can arrange their own shipping. Confirm the asset location, loading requirements and destination when you request a quotation.

What documentation is available for a used plant?

Documentation varies by asset. We share the equipment lists, drawings, process descriptions and inspection records available for each plant or stock number.

Is financing available?

Yes. IPP advertises financing for its plants and equipment. Contact IPP to discuss terms for your purchase.

Does IPP sell new chemical process equipment?

Yes. Gale Process Solutions (GPS), an IPP group company, fabricates new stainless steel process equipment with 12 to 16 week average delivery. Universal Glasteel Equipment (UGE) supplies new and re-glassed glass-lined equipment.

Does IPP buy shutdown chemical plants and surplus equipment?

Yes. We buy complete plants, process lines and individual equipment from shutdown, idled and restructured sites. Start with the sell a plant or sell equipment form.

What information should I provide when selling?

Provide an asset list, photographs, the plant location, available technical documents and your removal deadline. Our team reviews the information and confirms the next steps.

Does IPP buy the land and buildings with a chemical plant?

Yes. IPP purchases complete plant sites, including the land, buildings, equipment and intellectual property. We also buy individual process units and equipment systems within a site. Read how selling to IPP works.

Does IPP assume environmental obligations at a plant site?

Yes, in some acquisitions. IPP buys sites with known contamination and assumes site cleanup and regulatory compliance obligations as the buyer. Our remediation partners work with local environmental authorities on each site. Discuss a site exit with IPP.

Which companies sell chemical plants to IPP?

Fortune 100 and Fortune 500 companies typically represent 95% of the sellers IPP works with, according to IPP. IPP occasionally buys assets from smaller manufacturers in distressed situations.

Does IPP buy equipment as-is, where-is?

Yes. IPP buys used, surplus and idled equipment as-is, where-is, as a principal buyer rather than a broker. Our team arranges inspection, dismantling, packaging, freight and export.

Can IPP market a plant instead of buying it outright?

Yes. IPP proposes either an outright purchase or a marketing arrangement that offers the assets to its global buyer network. IPP markets BASF’s ammonia, methanol and melamine plants at Ludwigshafen under such an agreement.

Does IPP handle decommissioning and dismantling?

Yes. Our team decommissions and dismantles the plant, then packs, ships and exports the equipment. IPP holds ISN RAVS Plus verification for plant decommissioning work.

What happens to a chemical plant site after a sale?

The purchase agreement defines the outcome. A sale can cover equipment for removal or the entire site, and our published projects include equipment relocation and site repurposing.
