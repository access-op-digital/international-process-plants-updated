## High-Speed Dispersers vs High-Shear Mixers

Both machines break agglomerates in a liquid, and the open blade versus the enclosed rotor-stator head separates them.

| Factor | High-Speed Disperser | High-Shear Mixer |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Head | An open saw-tooth disc, the Cowles-style blade | A multi-blade rotor inside a slotted stator |
| Action | A strong vortex pulls powders into the teeth for de-lumping and wetting | Material is drawn into the rotor and forced out through the stator slots |
| Tip Speed | Commonly 12.7 to 25.4 m/s (2,500 to 5,000 ft/min) | Set by the rotor and the stator gap |
| Viscosity | Medium to high, up to 50,000 cP | Low to medium |
| Emulsions | Light emulsions | Fine, stable emulsions |
| In IPP's Inventory | Morehouse-COWLES, Drais, Bowers and Fluid units | The Fryma VME-500 homogenizer and the Abbe Dispersall's high shear mixer |

**Q: How does a high-speed disperser work?**
A: A motor turns a saw-tooth disc near the bottom of the shaft at tip speeds of 12.7 to 25.4 m/s (2,500 to 5,000 ft/min). The disc pulls liquid and powder from above and below into its teeth and throws them outward, so the vortex wets the powder and the impact breaks agglomerates apart; dissolver is another name for the same design.

**Q: What is a Cowles mixer?**
A: Cowles mixer names a high-speed disperser fitted with a Cowles blade: a saw-tooth disc whose serrated teeth alternate around its rim, called after Morehouse-COWLES dissolvers. Cowles-style blades run from 25 mm (1 in) lab sizes to 1,016 mm (40 in) production blades, usually in stainless steel 304 or 316, and IPP's Morehouse-COWLES EHD15 carries a 203 mm (8 in) stainless impeller.

**Q: Which dispersers work under vacuum?**
A: The Abbe VP Dispersall vessel is rated for full vacuum and 0.28 bar (4 psi), the Fryma VME-500 reaches 95% vacuum with a jacket rated 3 bar (43.5 psi), and the Buhler SMP-50-TS-S/V runs under a vacuum and dust lid.

**Q: What vessel sizes, speeds and motors are available?**
A: Vessels run from the Fryma's 500 L (132 gal) and the Abbe's 566 L (150 gal) to the Joshua Greaves system's 11,000 L (2,906 gal), and the Drais RSV-24ex takes 250 to 600 L (66 to 159 gal) bowls. Motors run from 2.6 kW (3.5 HP) on the Fluid SM-31 to 75 kW (100.6 HP) on the Joshua Greaves drive, with shaft speeds to 2,150 rpm on the Morehouse-COWLES EHD15.

**Q: Which dispersers have hydraulic or pneumatic lifts?**
A: The Morehouse-COWLES EHD15 rides a pneumatic lift that needs 6.9 bar (100 psi) of clean, dry air, the Drais RSV-24ex and Bowers 5VSM18 use hydraulic and air/oil lifts, and the Fluid SM-31 raises and lowers by hand. The Fryma VME-500 lifts its vessel cover hydraulically.
