## Conical vs Cylindrical Ball Mills

Conical and cylindrical ball mills both grind with a tumbling charge of balls, while shell shape sets how the charge sorts itself. Shell, charge, discharge and stock appear in the rows below.

| Factor | Conical | Cylindrical |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Shell | A short cylinder with cone sections at each end | A straight cylinder with flat or dished heads |
| Ball Charge | Large balls gather in the wide center and smaller balls toward the discharge cone | Balls mix along the full length |
| Grinding Action | Coarse grinding in the center, finer grinding toward the outlet | Uniform grinding along the shell |
| Operation | Continuous feed and discharge | Batch or continuous |
| In IPP's Inventory | Hardinge conical mills of 2,150 mm (84.1 in) | Blaw Knox mills of 1,850 mm (72 in) by 3,050 mm (120 in) |

**Q: How does a ball mill work?**
A: A ball mill turns a horizontal shell partly filled with steel balls, and the rotation lifts the balls until they cascade back onto the product. Impact breaks the coarse particles and attrition between the balls grinds them finer, so particle size falls as the feed moves from the inlet to the discharge. Grinding runs wet or dry depending on the machine design.

**Q: What is a vibratory ball mill?**
A: A vibratory ball mill shakes its grinding tube instead of turning it: an unbalanced exciter drives the tube in circular or elliptical motion, and the media grind the product through rapid impacts. IPP's TEMA Siebtechnik ESM 234-1 BS is an eccentric vibrating mill with a 19 L grinding tube that accepts rods, balls or pebbles.

**Q: What linings do the mills carry?**
A: The Blaw Knox mills carry Ni-hard linings for their steel ball charge, and the Engineering Plastics EPJ-100 chamber is lined with hard plastic and jacketed for water. The TEMA tube, which grinds wet or dry, and the rod mill are unlined stainless steel, so the TEMA also runs as a wet ball mill.

**Q: What sizes and drive powers are available?**
