## Graphite Block vs Shell and Tube Heat Exchangers

A graphite block exchanger routes both fluids through drilled channels in solid graphite, while a shell and tube exchanger carries one fluid inside a tube bundle and the other around it. Construction, flow paths, wetted material and modularity separate them in the table below.

| Factor | Graphite Block | Shell and Tube |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Construction | Impervious graphite blocks with drilled cross-flow channels, held in a steel shell or between end plates | A tube bundle with tubesheets inside a cylindrical shell |
| Flow Paths | Two sets of channels cross inside each block without meeting | One fluid in the tubes, the other on the shell side across baffles |
| Wetted Material | Resin-impregnated graphite | Carbon steel, stainless steel, alloys or graphite tubes |
| Modularity | Stacked blocks set the heat transfer area | Tube count and length set the heat transfer area |
| In IPP's Inventory | Ralph Coidan, Carbone Lorraine, Vicarb, GAB Neumann and SGL Carbon blocks | [Listed on IPP's shell and tube heat exchanger page](https://internationalprocessplants.com/process-equipment/heat-exchanger/shell-and-tube/) |

**Q: How does a graphite block heat exchanger work?**
A: A graphite block heat exchanger passes two fluids through separate sets of holes drilled in blocks of impervious, resin-impregnated graphite. The process holes run along the block and the service holes cross them at right angles, so heat moves through the graphite wall between the channels while the fluids never touch. This impervious graphite heat exchanger design, also called a carbon block heat exchanger, stacks blocks inside a steel shell or between end plates.

**Q: What is a cubic block heat exchanger?**
A: Cubic block heat exchangers build the exchanger from cube-shaped graphite blocks drilled in two directions and clamped together between steel plates, while cylindrical designs stack round blocks inside a shell. IMS lists cubic blocks from Robert Jenkins and Karbate in IPP's inventory.

**Q: Which other designs does the block category include?**
