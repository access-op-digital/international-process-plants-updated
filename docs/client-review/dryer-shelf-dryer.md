## Vacuum Shelf Dryers vs Hot Air Tray Dryers

Both designs hold the product on trays inside a cabinet; the heat path and the vapor route separate them. Factor by factor, a vacuum shelf dryer and a hot air tray dryer compare as follows.

| Factor | Vacuum Shelf Dryer | Hot Air Tray Dryer |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Heating | Hollow shelves heated by steam, hot water, oil or electricity conduct heat into the trays | Fans blow heated air across the trays (forced convection) |
| Pressure | A sealed chamber under vacuum lowers the boiling point of the moisture or solvent | Atmospheric pressure, with higher drying temperatures |
| Vapor | A vacuum pump draws the vapor off, and a condenser recovers solvents | Humid air exhausts to atmosphere |
| Construction | Vacuum-tight chamber, door seals, vacuum pump and condenser | Cabinet with fans and heaters |
| In IPP's Inventory | Vacuum-rated units from Devine, Zschokke Wartmann, United McGill, Italvacuum and others | The Apex B21E electric fan-heated tray dryer |

**Q: How does a vacuum shelf dryer work?**
A: A vacuum shelf dryer holds the product on trays that rest on hollow, heated shelves inside a sealed chamber. A vacuum pump lowers the chamber pressure, which drops the boiling point of the moisture or solvent, and heat from the shelves reaches each tray by conduction. The vapor leaves through the vacuum line to a condenser, and the dry product stays on the trays until the vacuum is released.

**Q: Is a vacuum shelf dryer the same as a vacuum tray dryer?**
A: Yes. Both names describe one design, a sealed vacuum chamber whose heated shelves carry trays of product, and listings use the terms interchangeably. Atmospheric tray dryers differ: fans blow hot air across the trays instead.

**Q: How are the shelves heated?**
A: Each unit lists its own heating medium: steam-cored shelves heat the Gruenberg, Leybold and N Lebel Pantin dryers, a liquid medium circulates through the vinyl-coated Devine shelves and the Edward King Zurich system, the Calmic system circulates hot oil, and the Hering, Leybold Heraeus and Apex units heat electrically.
