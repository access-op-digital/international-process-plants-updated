## Kady Mills vs Rotor Sieve Mills

Rotor-stator dispersion works on solids in a liquid, and screen sieving works on dry powder. Feed, mechanism, energy and stock line the two machines up in the table below.

| Factor | Kady Mill | Rotor Sieve Mill |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Feed | Solids in a liquid: slurries, fluids and high-viscosity mixes | Dry or semi-dry powders and granules |
| Mechanism | A slotted rotor throws the batch into a slotted stator at tip speeds up to 45.7 m/s (9,000 ft/min) | A rotor or blades sweep the product across a stationary perforated screen |
| Energy Input | High energy density in the close rotor-stator gap | Low to moderate, with the screen opening setting the particle size |
| Result | Wetted solids dispersed through the liquid | De-lumped, sized powder |
| In IPP's Inventory | Sourced to specification on request | Powtec RTS 350 x 300 in stainless steel 304 |

**Q: How does a Kady mill work?**
A: A Kady mill, a high-speed rotor-stator dispersion mill, draws the batch into a rotor that works as a centrifugal pump and throws the material through radial slots into the staggered slots of a stationary stator at tip speeds up to 45.7 m/s (9,000 ft/min). Impact against the stator and hydraulic shear in the close gap fracture agglomerates and wet the new surfaces, so the solids spread through the liquid.

**Q: What is a rotor sieve mill?**
A: A rotor sieve mill turns a rotor or a set of blades against a stationary perforated screen, and impact and shear reduce the product until its particles pass the screen openings. The sieve aperture sets the final particle size, and the Powtec RTS 350 x 300 drives its 8-blade rotor over a stainless steel 304 sieve.
