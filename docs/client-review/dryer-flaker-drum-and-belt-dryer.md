## Drum Flakers vs Belt Flakers

A drum flaker solidifies or dries the feed on a rotating drum, while a belt flaker cools it on a moving steel belt. The table compares the two machine types on each factor.

| Factor | Drum | Belt |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Contact Surface | A rotating drum, chilled for flaking or steam-heated for drying | A moving stainless steel belt, cooled by water sprayed on its underside |
| Feed | A dip tray, feed pan, applicator roll or submerged feed roll | A drop former (pastillator), a dosing system or calender rolls onto the belt |
| Product Form | Thin flakes cut from the drum by a knife | Pastilles, or flakes broken from the solidified layer at the belt end |
| Footprint | Compact, with drum faces up to 1,550 mm (60.6 in) in stock | Long, with belts up to 24,850 mm (978 in) in stock |
| In IPP's Inventory | Simon Dryers, APV Mitchell, Blaw Knox, Gouda, Escher Wyss, Stokes and Bethlehem units | Sandvik, Berndorf, Kaiser, Steel Belt Systems, Gunter Stenger, North American Process Systems and Maschinenbau Technik units |

**Q: How does a drum flaker work?**
A: A drum flaker turns a hollow drum against a liquid or molten feed, so a thin layer coats the drum surface as it rotates. Cooling water inside the drum solidifies a melt, or steam inside it dries a liquid feed, and a knife scrapes the layer off as flakes before the drum completes its turn. Drum speed and feed level set the flake thickness on each flaker machine.

**Q: How does a belt flaker cool the product?**
A: A belt flaker carries the melt on a thin stainless steel belt, a cooling belt conveyor running between two drums, while cooling water sprays the underside of the belt through one or more cooling zones. The product sets before the far drum, where a scraper lifts it off as flakes or releases the pastilles. IPP's Sandvik unit runs four cooling zones of 49 nozzles each, and the larger Steel Belt Systems flaker three zones of 60.

**Q: What is a pastillator?**
A: Pastillators turn a melt into uniform drops, or pastilles, on a steel belt cooler: a drop former such as a Rotoform deposits the melt in rows on the moving belt, and water sprayed under the belt solidifies each drop. Every pastillator machine in IPP's stock pairs a drop former or dosing system with a cooling belt, and the pastillation systems here come from Sandvik, Berndorf, Kaiser and Maschinenbau Technik.

**Q: Is a drum flaker the same as a drum dryer?**
A: Not quite. Both coat a rotating drum with the feed and remove it with a knife, but a drum flaker mainly cools a melt until it solidifies into flakes, while a drum dryer evaporates water from a slurry on a steam-heated drum. Several units here, cored for steam or hot oil, run as flaker dryers, and IPP lists dedicated drum dryers on its [drum dryer page](/process-equipment/dryer/drum-dryer/).

**Q: What sizes are available?**
