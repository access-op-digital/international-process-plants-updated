## Desiccant vs Refrigerated Air Dryers

Desiccant dryers adsorb water vapor onto a drying agent, while refrigerated dryers condense it out by chilling the air, and the two methods reach different dew points. The table compares them factor by factor.

| Factor | Desiccant | Refrigerated |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Drying Method | Desiccant beads adsorb water vapor from the compressed air | Refrigeration cools the air so the water condenses, then the air is rewarmed |
| Pressure Dew Point | -40 °C (-40 °F) down to -73 °C (-100 °F) | 2 °C (35 °F) to 4 °C (40 °F) |
| Regeneration | Purge air, heaters or a blower dry the off-line tower | None: a drain removes the condensate |
| Energy Use | Purge air or heater energy | Power for the refrigeration compressor |

**Q: How does a desiccant air dryer work?**
A: A desiccant air dryer passes compressed air through a tower of desiccant beads, and the beads adsorb the water vapor so dry air leaves the tower. A second tower regenerates at the same time, driving off the captured moisture, and a valve system switches the towers on a timed cycle. That working principle keeps the air system supplied with dry air without stopping the flow.

**Q: What is the difference between heatless and heated desiccant dryers?**
A: Both are regenerative designs that differ in how the off-line tower dries out. A heatless dryer diverts 15% to 20% of its own dry compressed air through the saturated tower as purge air, while a heated dryer warms the purge air with an electric or steam heater, or a blower, and cuts purge use to 5% to 7%. IPP's Arrow RH212A runs as a heatless desiccant air dryer.

**Q: What dew point does a desiccant dryer reach?**
A: Desiccant dryers reach pressure dew points from -40 °C (-40 °F) down to -73 °C (-100 °F), far below the 2 °C (35 °F) to 4 °C (40 °F) of a refrigerated dryer. The rating of a specific unit depends on its design and operating conditions, so IPP confirms it on the data sheet.
