## Direct vs Indirect Rotary Dryers

Direct and indirect designs both turn a horizontal drum to move the product, and the heat path inside each dryer divides them. The rows below trace those differences.

| Factor | Direct (Hot Air) | Indirect |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Heat Transfer | Hot gas or heated air flows through the drum and touches the product | Heat passes through the shell or through internal steam or thermal fluid tubes |
| Heat Path | Convection | Conduction and radiation through a metal wall |
| Product Contact | Flights lift and cascade the product through the gas stream | The product stays separate from the heating gas |
| Exhaust | Larger gas volumes, with exhaust and dust collection systems | Smaller exhaust gas volumes |
| Thermal Efficiency | Higher, since the gas meets the wet solids directly | Lower, since the heat must cross a metal wall |

**Q: What do the flights and knockers on a rotary dryer do?**
A: Flights are plates along the inside of the drum that lift the product and shower it through the hot air stream, which speeds heat transfer and moves the product toward the discharge. Knockers strike the outside of the shell to keep the product flowing freely in the feed end. IPP's refurbished stainless steel 316 dryer carries six sets of 12 flights, product discharge fingers and external knockers.

**Q: What is the difference between co-current and counter-current rotary dryers?**
A: In a co-current dryer, the product and the hot gas travel in the same direction, so the wettest product meets the hottest gas at the inlet and evaporation cools it. In a counter-current dryer, they travel in opposite directions, the driest product meets the hottest gas at the outlet, and the exhaust passes its heat to the incoming wet feed. IMS records co-current flow on IPP's refurbished stainless steel 316 dryer and the Link Belt unit.

**Q: How are IPP's rotary dryers heated?**
A: IMS records steam heating on the Link Belt, Standard Steel and refurbished stainless steel 316 dryers: five steam hot air coils warm the air on the Link Belt unit, and the refurbished dryer carries a steam chest rated for 75 kW (256,000 BTU/hr). The Patterson Kelly tube-type unit lists a 260 °C (500 °F) maximum tube operating temperature.
