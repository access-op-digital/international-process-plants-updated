## Two-Roll Mills vs Roll Crushers

Both machines pull material into the nip between two counter-rotating rolls, and roll speed, feed, output and temperature control set the two apart.

| Factor | Two-Roll Mill | Roll Crusher |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Roll Speeds | Different surface speeds, set by the friction ratio | Rolls turn toward each other to grip and draw the feed downward |
| Action | Shear and kneading in the nip | Compression and shear that fracture the lumps |
| Feed | Rubber and plastic compounds, polymers and dry blends | Lumps and coarse solids dropped from a hopper |
| Output | A mixed sheet that bands around the slower roll | Crushed fragments that fall from the gap, sized by the roll gap |
| Roll Temperature | Hollow rolls heated or cooled by circulating fluid | Not stated on the Gundlach listing |
| In IPP's Inventory | Farrel, lab and production units | Gundlach 4080S with 610 mm (24 in) rolls |

**Q: How does a two-roll mill work?**
A: Material enters the nip between two hollow rolls that turn toward each other at different surface speeds, and the speed difference shears and squeezes it on every pass. The compound bands around the slower front roll, where the operator cuts and folds it back into the nip until the batch is uniform and then takes it off as a sheet, while fluid circulating inside the rolls holds their temperature and the nip gap sets the sheet thickness.

**Q: Is a rubber mill the same as a two-roll mill?**
A: Yes. Rubber mill, rubber mixing mill and open mill are trade names for the two-roll mill, whose two horizontally opposed rolls turn toward each other at different speeds to mix a compound. Edwin Chaffee developed the two-roll rubber mill in the 1830s.

**Q: What does a 1.4:1 friction ratio mean?**
A: A 1.4:1 friction ratio means one roll's surface travels 1.4 times as fast as the other's. The speed difference shears and squeezes the material in the nip and keeps the compound on the slower roll for cutting and folding; the chrome-plated lab mills in IPP's stock, the Farrel among them, run at 1.4:1.

**Q: Does IPP have lab-size two-roll mills?**
A: Yes. IPP's stock includes chrome-plated lab two-roll mills with 152.4 mm (6 in) by 330.2 mm (13 in) rolls, micrometer roll adjustment and 5.6 kW (7.5 HP) motors on 208 V 60 Hz power, plus a stand-mounted chrome mill with 76.2 mm (3 in) by 203.2 mm (8 in) rolls at 30 rpm and its own controls.

**Q: Which roll mills have heated or cooled rolls?**
A: The 76.2 mm (3 in) chrome mill has rolls cored for steam heating at 1 bar (15 psi) and water cooling at 3.4 bar (50 psi). The Farrel mill's rolls are cored for heating and rated 148.9 °C (300 °F), the unused mill's rolls are cored for steam heating, and the carbon steel mill's rolls are cored for cooling through a rotary joint.

**Q: What are the Gundlach 4080S roll crusher's specifications?**
