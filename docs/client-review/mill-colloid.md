## Colloid Mills vs High-Pressure Homogenizers

Both machines reduce droplet and particle size in liquid products, and the force each one applies sets them apart. Mechanism, pressure, feed and stock line up in the rows below.

| Factor | Colloid Mill | High-Pressure Homogenizer |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Mechanism | Shear in the narrow gap between a fast rotor and a stator | A piston pump forces the product through an adjustable valve gap |
| Operating Pressure | Low: the rotor moves the product | Hundreds of bar at the valve |
| Feed | Pastes and viscous products as well as liquids | Pumpable liquids |
| Adjustment | Rotor-to-stator gap setting | Homogenizing pressure setting |
| In IPP's Inventory | IKA-Werk, Premier and a jacketed stainless steel 316 unit | [Listed on IPP's homogenizer page](/process-equipment/homogenizer/) |

**Q: How does a colloid mill work?**
A: A colloid mill feeds the product between a rotor spinning at high speed and a stationary stator set a fraction of a millimeter away. The intense shear in that gap tears droplets and agglomerates apart, so the product leaves as a fine emulsion or dispersion, and the gap setting controls the result. The name colloidal mill describes the same machine and working principle.

**Q: Why does the category include attritors and hammer mills?**
A: IMS groups size-reduction machines for fine milling under its colloid mill category, so the listing also carries Peters & Smith attritors and Alpine (Hosokawa), Williams, Reitz and Bepex hammer and impact mills beside the rotor-stator units. The type filter on this page separates them.

**Q: What is an attritor?**
A: Attritors grind with a stirred bed of small media: a vertical shaft with arms turns inside a stationary tank filled with steel shot or balls, and the moving media grind the product by impact and shear. IPP's Peters & Smith attritors run 0.42 m³ (14.7 ft³) of chrome steel shot at 51 rpm in jacketed stainless steel 304 tanks.

**Q: What motors and speeds are available?**

**Q: What are the mills made of?**
