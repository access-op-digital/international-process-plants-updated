## Banbury Mixers vs Amalgamators

Both machines mix heavy batches, and the Banbury's closed rotor chamber against the amalgamator's twin sigma blades separates them.

| Factor | Banbury Mixer | Amalgamator |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Mixing Elements | Two winged rotors turning in opposite directions at slightly different speeds | Two heavy-duty sigma blades |
| Feed | Polymers, fillers, oils and additives through a top hopper | Pellets with liquid and powder additives |
| Action | High shear and kneading that heat and homogenize the compound | Blending the additives evenly through the pellets |

**Q: How does a Banbury mixer work?**
A: Raw polymer, fillers, oils and additives drop through a top hopper, and a pneumatic or hydraulic ram pushes them down into a closed internal chamber. Two winged rotors turn in opposite directions at slightly different speeds, shearing and kneading the batch until the heat of mixing softens it into a uniform compound, and a drop door or slide at the bottom then releases it.

**Q: What does an amalgamator do?**
A: An industrial amalgamator mixes pellets with liquid and powder additives, typically with two heavy-duty sigma blades, so the additives spread evenly through the batch. IPP's stock holds a Mazzoni MSB-1600 of this type.
