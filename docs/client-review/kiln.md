## Indirect-Fired vs Direct-Fired Rotary Kilns

In an indirect-fired kiln, burners or heaters warm the outside of the rotating tube, while a direct-fired kiln sends hot combustion gas through the tube itself. Heat path, atmosphere and tube build differ in the rows below.

| Factor | Indirect-Fired | Direct-Fired |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Heat Path | Heat passes through the tube wall from an external furnace or heaters | Hot gas contacts the material inside the tube |
| Atmosphere | Isolated from combustion gas, so the internal atmosphere stays controlled | Mixed with combustion gas |
| Tube | Alloy tubes such as Inconel, unlined | Steel shells, often refractory-lined |
| Scale | Smaller tube diameters | Larger diameters and throughputs |

**Q: How does an indirect-fired rotary kiln work?**
A: An indirect-fired rotary kiln turns an alloy tube slowly inside an insulated furnace, and burners or electric heaters in the furnace heat the outside of the tube. The heat passes through the tube wall into the solids, which tumble and advance along the slight slope from the feed end to the discharge end; this indirect process keeps the material apart from any combustion gas.

**Q: Is a rotary calciner the same as a rotary kiln?**
A: Rotary calciners belong to the rotary kiln family: the term usually means a kiln, often indirect-fired, that heats solids to drive off water, carbon dioxide or other volatiles at high temperature. IPP's Bethlehem indirect-fired kiln follows that layout, with an Inconel tube rated to 1,100 °C (2,000 °F).

**Q: What are the kiln tubes made of?**
