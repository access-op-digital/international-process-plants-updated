## Pebble Mills vs Ball Mills

Both mill types share the tumbling principle, while media, lining and charge weight separate them.

| Factor | Pebble Mill | Ball Mill |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Grinding Media | Flint pebbles, porcelain or high-alumina ceramic | Forged steel, iron or stainless steel balls |
| Lining | Ceramic brick, porcelain or rubber | Alloy steel or metal liners |
| Charge Weight | Lighter media, so the mill draws less power and needs a larger shell for the same output | Media over three times heavier for the same volume, for faster grinding |
| Metal Contact | No metal media or lining touches the product | Steel media and shell touch the product |

**Q: How does a pebble mill work?**
A: A pebble mill turns a horizontal shell filled halfway with flint pebbles or ceramic balls and the product, wet or dry. The rotation lifts the charge until it cascades back, impact and attrition between the tumbling pebbles grind the product, and the design keeps metal out of the grinding zone with a ceramic, porcelain or rubber lining.

**Q: Is a ceramic ball mill the same as a pebble mill?**
A: Yes. Ceramic ball mills and ceramic-lined ball mills are pebble mills under other names: ball mills whose shells are lined with ceramic, porcelain or rubber and whose media are ceramic balls or flint pebbles instead of steel.

**Q: What is a jar mill?**
A: A jar mill rolls sealed cylindrical jars of grinding media on powered rollers, so the tumbling media wet- or dry-grind, blend or mix small batches. Bench and floor models hold one jar or tiers of jars, and with porcelain jars and ceramic media a jar mill works as a small pebble mill.

**Q: What should I send IPP to source a pebble mill?**
A: Send the shell diameter and length, the lining and media you need, the batch volume, wet or dry duty, and the drive and power supply at your site. Candidate units come back with data sheets, photographs and pricing.

**Q: Which mills in IPP's inventory take pebble media?**
