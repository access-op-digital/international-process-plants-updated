## Bead Mills vs Attritors

Both machines grind with agitated media, and media size, shaft speed and chamber design separate them.

| Factor | Bead Mill | Attritor |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Design | A sealed horizontal or vertical chamber with discs or pins on a fast shaft | A stationary tank with a central shaft whose arms stir the media |
| Media | Small beads of 0.1 to 3 mm (0.004 to 0.12 in) | Media of 1 mm (0.04 in) and up, including balls |
| Shaft Speed | High-speed agitation | Slow running, at circumferential speeds of 0.5 to 2 m/s (1.6 to 6.6 ft/s) |
| Operation | Continuous pass or recirculation, with a screen or gap holding the beads | Batch or continuous |
| In IPP's Inventory | Netzsch, WAB Dyno-Mill, Asada, FrymaKoruma and Drais mills | Union Process Szegvari bench-top and 100S units |

**Q: How does a bead mill work?**
A: A bead mill fills a closed chamber partly with small grinding media, and discs, pins or a rotor on a fast shaft set the beads in motion. The colliding beads grind and disperse the solids in a wet slurry by impact, compression and shear, a separator screen or gap keeps the beads in the chamber as the product leaves, and a cooling jacket carries off the heat of grinding.

**Q: Is a sand mill the same as a bead mill?**
A: Yes, in practice. Sand mill is the older name, from the silica sand once used as grinding media, and bead mill is the current name for the same wet-grinding machine, which now runs engineered beads of glass, ceramic or zirconia; IMS files these units as shot and sand mills.
