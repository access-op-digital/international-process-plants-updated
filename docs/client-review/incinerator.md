## Flameless vs Direct-Fired Thermal Oxidizers

Both designs destroy organic vapors by oxidation at high temperature, and each oxidizer type runs that reaction in a different zone. Oxidation zone, heat source, flame and feed separate the two in the rows below.

| Factor | Flameless | Direct-Fired |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Oxidation Zone | A heated bed of inert ceramic media | A burner flame in a combustion chamber |
| Heat Source | Preheaters bring the bed to temperature, and the oxidation heat then sustains the reaction | Fuel burners fire continuously |
| Flame | No visible flame in the oxidation zone | An open burner flame |
| Feed | Premixed waste gas and air enter the media bed | Waste gas, fuel and air pass through the burner flame |

**Q: How does a flameless thermal oxidizer work?**
A: A flameless thermal oxidizer passes the process gas through a packed bed of hot ceramic media, where the organic vapors meet oxygen and oxidize to carbon dioxide and water inside the bed. Because the reaction stays within the bed, no flame burns in the oxidation zone.

**Q: Is a thermal oxidizer the same as an industrial incinerator?**
A: Not exactly. An industrial incinerator burns solid, liquid or gaseous waste, while a thermal oxidizer treats an air or gas stream, destroying its organic vapors by oxidation at high temperature. IMS files IPP's Thermatrix ES-100, a gas-only flameless oxidizer, under its incinerator category.

**Q: Why does the ES-100 use electric heating elements?**
A: The ES-100 has no burner: its electric heating elements preheat the ceramic bed to the temperature at which oxidation starts, the approach of an electric thermal oxidizer. Once the vapors oxidize, their own heat release helps hold the bed at temperature.

**Q: What capacity and ratings does the ES-100 carry?**
A: The ES-100 thermal oxidizer unit carries 73.9 kW (252,000 BTU/h) of capacity, 237.9 m³/h (140 CFM) of gas flow, a combustion chamber area of 3.9 m² (42 ft²) and a 10.7 m (35 ft) stack, rated 0.07 bar (1 psi) at 204.4 °C (400 °F).
