## Air-Cooled vs Water-Cooled Refrigerated Dryers

Every refrigerated dryer rejects the heat it removes through a condenser, and the condenser's cooling medium sets the two designs apart. The table compares air-cooled and water-cooled units.

| Factor | Air-Cooled | Water-Cooled |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Condenser Cooling | A fan pushes ambient air across the condenser | Cooling water flows through the condenser |
| Utilities | Electrical power only | Electrical power plus a cooling water supply and return |
| Heat Rejection | Releases the heat into the surrounding room | Carries the heat away in the cooling water |
| Room Ventilation | Needs air movement around the cabinet | Depends less on room ventilation |
| In IPP's Inventory | Ingersoll Rand TS 2000, rated 13.9 kW | Ingersoll Rand DXR 2000W |

**Q: How does a refrigerated air dryer work?**
A: A refrigerated dryer cools the compressed air in a heat exchanger chilled by a refrigeration circuit, so the water vapor condenses into liquid. A separator collects the condensate and a drain removes it, and the cold, dry air then passes back through an air-to-air exchanger that rewarms it before it leaves. That working principle keeps the outlet air above its dew point, so moisture does not condense in the downstream lines.

**Q: How does a refrigerated dryer differ from a desiccant dryer?**
A: A refrigerated dryer condenses water vapor by chilling the air, which delivers a pressure dew point of 2 °C (35 °F) to 4 °C (40 °F), while a desiccant dryer adsorbs the vapor onto a drying agent and reaches -40 °C (-40 °F) to -73 °C (-100 °F). IPP lists PneumaTech, Arrow and Sullair units on its [desiccant air dryer page](/process-equipment/dryer/desiccant/).
