## Pan Dryers vs Rotary Vacuum Dryers

Pan dryers and rotary vacuum dryers both dry a batch by conduction from a heated jacket under vacuum, while vessel orientation and agitator layout separate the designs, factor by factor below.

| Factor | Pan Design | Rotary Vacuum Design |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Vessel | A vertical, round pan with a flat or slightly conical heated bottom | A horizontal cylindrical shell |
| Agitator | Blades or ploughs on a vertical shaft, driven from the top or the bottom | Paddles or breaker arms on a central horizontal shaft |
| Mixing Action | The agitator wipes the heated floor and lifts the product | The paddles tumble the batch and break wet cake along the shell |
| Heating | Steam, hot water or thermal fluid in the jacketed wall and bottom, often in the agitator too | A heating medium in the shell jacket, sometimes in the agitator shaft |
| Discharge | A side discharge valve in the lower vessel wall | Reversed agitator rotation pushes product to a bottom outlet |

**Q: How does a vacuum pan dryer work?**
A: A vacuum pan dryer heats a batch through a jacket on the pan wall and bottom while a vacuum pump lowers the pressure inside the sealed vessel. Under that reduced pressure, water and solvents evaporate at a lower product temperature, and the vapor passes to a condenser where solvent is recovered. This working principle keeps the product temperature low, and the dry batch leaves through a discharge valve once it reaches its target moisture.

**Q: What is an agitated pan dryer?**
A: Inside an agitated pan dryer, blades or ploughs turn the batch during drying, so fresh product keeps meeting the heated floor and wall and lumps break up. Run under vacuum, the same machine carries the vacuum pan name, and at the end of the batch the agitator sweeps the dry product toward the discharge valve.
