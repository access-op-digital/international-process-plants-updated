## What Is Re-Glassing and Why Does It Matter?

Re-glassing restores a glass-lined drive agitator to factory-spec performance at a fraction of the cost of a new OEM unit. IPP stocks over 365 re-glassed glass-lined agitators ready for immediate delivery.

### IPP Re-Glasses Glass-Lined Drives to Factory Specification

- Strip old glass lining — IPP removes the worn or damaged borosilicate glass from the OEM drive housing
- Inspect metal substrate — the base metal housing is inspected for corrosion, pitting, or dimensional issues
- Apply new borosilicate glass — fresh glass coating is applied to all wetted surfaces using OEM-spec materials
- Fire in kiln — the coated housing is fired at controlled temperatures to fuse the glass to the metal substrate
- Spark test and certify — holiday detection testing at up to 12 kV verifies lining integrity. Lining thickness is measured and documented.
Re-glassing is the process of stripping the old borosilicate glass from an OEM drive housing, applying a new glass coating, firing it in a kiln at controlled temperatures, and spark testing the finished lining to detect any holidays or pinholes. The result is a glass-lined drive with a corrosion barrier equivalent to a new Pfaudler or DeDietrich unit.

IPP re-glasses drives using the same borosilicate glass formulation and firing process as the original manufacturers. Every re-glassed drive from IPP passes holiday detection testing at up to 12 kV and includes a lining thickness report documenting the new glass coating.

IPP maintains the largest inventory of re-glassed glass-lined drive agitators in the used equipment market. Each unit has been restored to factory specification with new borosilicate glass, spark tested, and certified. You receive factory-spec performance at approximately half the cost of a new Pfaudler or DeDietrich drive.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Used Glass-Lined Drive Agitators

Answers to the most common questions from procurement teams evaluating used glass-lined drive agitators for sale.

**Q: What is a glass-lined drive agitator?**
A: A glass-lined drive agitator is a gearbox-and-motor assembly designed specifically for glass-lined reactors. The wetted parts — including the stuffing box, bearing housing, and shaft sealing area — are coated with borosilicate glass to create a corrosion barrier that prevents metal contamination in the process fluid. Glass-lined drives mount to the reactor cover via a flange connection and seal against the glass-lined vessel interior. Pfaudler and DeDietrich manufacture the most commonly used glass-lined drive models for pharmaceutical, fine chemical, and agrochemical applications.

**Q: What does re-glassed mean?**
A: Re-glassed means the original OEM drive housing (typically Pfaudler or DeDietrich) has been stripped of its old glass lining, coated with new borosilicate glass, fired in a kiln at controlled temperatures, and spark tested to verify lining integrity. The re-glassing process uses the same glass formulation and firing parameters as the original manufacturer. The result is a drive with a factory-equivalent glass corrosion barrier at about half the cost of a new OEM unit. IPP stocks over 365 re-glassed glass-lined agitators ready for delivery.

**Q: How is the glass lining inspected before sale?**
A: IPP inspects every glass-lined drive before sale using spark testing (holiday detection) at up to 12 kV. Spark testing passes a high-voltage probe across the glass surface — any pinhole, crack, or thin spot in the lining allows current to pass and triggers a spark, identifying the defect location. IPP also measures lining thickness at multiple points and performs visual inspection for chips, crazing, or surface damage. Buyers receive a spark test certificate and lining thickness report with every glass-lined drive purchase from IPP.

**Q: What is spark testing?**
A: Spark testing (also called holiday detection) is a non-destructive inspection method that verifies glass lining integrity. A high-voltage probe (up to 12 kV for glass linings) is passed across the glass-coated surface. If the glass lining is intact, no current passes through. If there is a pinhole, crack, or thin spot in the glass, current passes through to the metal substrate and produces a visible spark at the defect location. Spark testing is the standard inspection method for glass-lined process equipment and is performed on every glass-lined drive agitator sold by IPP.

**Q: How do I match a glass-lined drive to my Pfaudler reactor?**
A: Matching a glass-lined drive to your Pfaudler reactor requires your reactor model number, nozzle size, and cover configuration. Pfaudler drives use RW, TW, and DTW mounting designations that correspond to specific reactor cover geometries and nozzle sizes. The drive flange must match the reactor cover nozzle for proper sealing against the glass-lined interior. Provide your reactor nameplate data — including model, serial number, and nozzle size — and IPP will identify the correct replacement drive from our Pfaudler inventory at no charge.

**Q: Can I use a Pfaudler drive on a DeDietrich reactor?**
A: Cross-manufacturer compatibility between Pfaudler and DeDietrich glass-lined drives is not guaranteed. Each manufacturer uses its own mounting geometry, flange dimensions, and sealing configurations. While adapter plates can sometimes bridge the differences, IPP recommends matching the drive manufacturer to your reactor manufacturer for guaranteed bolt-on compatibility. IPP stocks Pfaudler and DeDietrich drives — contact IPP to verify compatibility before purchasing.

**Q: What power rating do I need for my glass-lined reactor?**

IPP stocks glass-lined drives from 0.75 kW (1 HP) for small pilot reactors up to 87.2 kW (117 HP) for large production-scale vessels. As a general reference: reactors up to 500 L typically use 0.75–3.7 kW (1–5 HP); reactors from 500–4,000 L typically use 3.7–15 kW (5–20 HP); reactors above 4,000 L may require 15–87.2 kW (20–117 HP). **Q: What is the difference between a glass-lined drive and a standard drive agitator?**
A: Glass-lined drive agitators feature borosilicate glass-coated wetted parts designed specifically for glass-lined reactors. The glass lining prevents metal contamination in corrosive pharmaceutical and chemical processes. Standard drive agitators use bare metal construction (carbon steel, stainless steel, or Hastelloy) and are suited for open tanks, stainless steel reactors, and non-glass-lined vessels where corrosion resistance comes from the shaft and impeller metallurgy rather than a glass coating. If your reactor is glass-lined, you need a glass-lined drive to maintain the corrosion barrier through the entire vessel assembly. **Q: When do I need a glass-lined vs bare metal agitator?**

The glass lining on the drive assembly extends the reactor's corrosion barrier through the agitator mounting area, preventing bare metal exposure to the process fluid. If your reactor is bare stainless steel, carbon steel, or Hastelloy, you use a [standard drive agitator](/equipment/agitator/drive/) or [shaft/mixer agitator](/equipment/agitator/shaft-mixer/) matched to the vessel metallurgy.
