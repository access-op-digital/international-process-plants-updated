## Underwater vs Strand Pelletizers

Each pelletizer type turns molten polymer into pellets, and the point where the knife meets the melt separates them. Cutting point, pellet shape, cooling and stock compare in the rows below.

| Factor | Underwater | Strand |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Cutting Point | Rotating knives cut the melt at the die face, inside a water-filled chamber | A cutter chops continuous strands after they cool |
| Pellet Shape | Spherical or lens-shaped | Cylindrical with flat ends |
| Cooling | Process water quenches the pellets and carries them to a centrifugal dryer | Strands harden in a water trough or cooling air before the cutter |
| System | A closed water loop with filtration, temperature control and a dryer | A water bath and a mechanical strand cutter |
| In IPP's Inventory | Black Clawson system with a Gala 3016-LD spin dryer | Rieter (Maag) M-USG 900 V underwater strand units in [IMS's plastics categories](https://ims.internationalprocessplants.com/search?q=pelletizer) |

A: No. IMS's pellet mill category lists an underwater pelletizing system and a centrifugal pelletizer rather than ring-die or flat-die pellet mills, and IPP's sourcing team locates a die pellet mill to your specification on request.

**Q: How does an underwater pelletizer work?**
A: An extruder or melt pump pushes molten polymer through a heated die plate into a water-filled cutting chamber, where knives turning against the die face slice the emerging strands into pellets. Circulating process water quenches the pellets and carries them to a centrifugal dryer that spins the water off, and the water returns through filtration and temperature control to close the loop of the pelletizing system.

**Q: What is a centrifugal pelletizer?**
A: In a centrifugal pelletizer, the melt enters a rotating die chamber at atmospheric pressure and the rotation drives it through the die holes, where a blade cuts the emerging material into pellets. IPP's Baker Perkins unit takes its feed through a 25 to 38 mm (1 to 1.5 in) inlet and carries an adjustable cutting blade, a cooling jacket on the die housing and an infrared temperature meter.

**Q: What comes with the Black Clawson pelletizing system?**
A: The line pairs the stainless steel 316 Black Clawson underwater pelletizer, with internal rotary knives, with a Trendelkamp TAV-4 diverter valve on a hydraulic power pack, a Kenics 300-HEX-915 shell and tube heat exchanger of 27.9 m² (300 ft²) and a MAAG Therminox TX-70/70 gear pump rated 3 m³/h (13.2 gpm) at 220 °C (428 °F). The downstream equipment includes a Gala 3016-LD centrifugal spin dryer with an agglomerate catcher, a Midwestern Industries two-deck vibratory screen of 1,200 mm (47 in), a 3,420 L (903 gal) slurry tank with its pump, a pellet blowing unit, control panels, pipework and filters.

**Q: What are the drive and die specifications?**
A: The Black Clawson pelletizer runs a 22 kW (29.5 HP) drive at 1,470 rpm, is rated 230 °C (446 °F) and cuts through a die plate with 1.98 mm (0.078 in) holes, and IMS lists the line at 2,727 kg/h (6,000 lb/h) for a molten feed of specific gravity 0.96. The Baker Perkins unit pairs a 3.7 kW (5 HP) motor at 3,500 rpm on 230/460 V 60 Hz power with a blade rated to 10,000 rpm and a die with 200 micron (0.008 in) outlets.
