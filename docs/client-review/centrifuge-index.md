## Frequently Asked Questions About Buying Used Centrifuges

Answers to the most common questions from team members and procurement teams evaluating used centrifuges for sale.

**Q: What types of used centrifuges does IPP have for sale?**
A: IPP stocks centrifuges across multiple subtypes: auto discharge-bottom basket centrifuges, manual discharge-top basket centrifuges, basket centrifuge parts, disc bowl centrifuges, inverting filter centrifuges, and solid bowl-decanter centrifuges. Manufacturers include Alfa Laval, Westfalia, GEA, Broadbent, Ferrum, Krauss Maffei, Heinkel, Bird, Humboldt Wedag, Tolhurst, DeLaval, and others. Browse the IMS inventory for current availability.

**Q: What is the difference between a disc bowl centrifuge and a basket centrifuge?**
A: Disc bowl centrifuges use a stack of conical discs rotating at high speed (up to 10,000 RPM) to separate liquids from liquids or fine solids from liquids through centrifugal sedimentation. They operate continuously and are ideal for clarification, liquid-liquid separation, and high-purity applications. Basket centrifuges use a perforated basket spinning at moderate speed to separate solids from liquids through centrifugal filtration. They operate in batch mode and are suited for solids dewatering, crystal washing, and cake discharge applications. IPP stocks disc bowl centrifuges and basket centrifuges across multiple configurations.

**Q: What is an inverting filter centrifuge?**
A: An inverting filter centrifuge is a basket centrifuge that uses an invertible filter cloth to discharge the product cake cleanly and automatically, without manual handling. The filter cloth inverts (turns inside out) to release the cake, then returns to its normal position for the next batch. These centrifuges are particularly suited for pharmaceutical and fine chemical applications where product contamination must be minimized. IPP stocks inverting filter centrifuges, all from Heinkel, in Hastelloy C-22, C-4, stainless steel 316, 316L, 316Ti, and lined construction.

**Q: What is a solid bowl-decanter centrifuge used for?**
A: Solid bowl-decanter centrifuges continuously separate solids from liquids by spinning a cylindrical bowl at high speed, with an internal screw conveyor transporting solids toward the discharge end. Primary uses include wastewater sludge dewatering, chemical process slurry separation, and biofuel production. IPP stocks decanter centrifuges from Bird, Westfalia, Alfa Laval, Humboldt Wedag, Centrisys, Pieralisi, and other manufacturers.

**Q: How does centrifugal separation work?**
A: Centrifugal separation uses high-speed rotation to generate centrifugal force many times greater than gravity. This accelerated force causes denser materials (solids or heavier liquids) to migrate outward while lighter materials (clarified liquid or lighter liquids) remain closer to the center. The two primary separation principles in centrifuges are sedimentation (used in disc bowl and decanter centrifuges) and filtration (used in basket centrifuges). Sedimentation relies on density differences between phases, while filtration passes liquid through a filter medium under centrifugal force.

**Q: What is G-force and why does it matter for centrifuge selection?**
A: G-force (relative centrifugal force) measures how many times the force of gravity is applied to the material being separated. Higher G-force means faster and more complete separation. In IPP's inventory, G-forces range from 541 on basket centrifuges to 1,900 on decanter centrifuges. Disc bowl centrifuges typically generate the highest G-forces for fine particle and liquid-liquid separation. Decanters generate moderate G-forces for slurry dewatering. Basket centrifuges generate lower G-forces but excel at filtration with cake washing.

**Q: What is the difference between centrifugal sedimentation and centrifugal filtration?**
A: In centrifugal sedimentation (disc bowl and decanter centrifuges), phases separate based on density differences alone, without a filter medium. The denser phase migrates to the bowl wall while the lighter phase is displaced inward. In centrifugal filtration (basket and inverting filter centrifuges), the slurry is fed into a basket with a perforated wall or filter cloth. Centrifugal force drives the liquid through the filter medium, retaining the solid cake on the basket wall. Sedimentation is suited for clarification and liquid-liquid separation. Filtration is suited for solids dewatering and cake washing.

**Q: How do I choose between batch and continuous centrifuge operation?**
A: Batch centrifuges (basket and inverting filter types) process a fixed volume per cycle and are suited for applications requiring cake washing, multiple product grades, or smaller batch volumes. Continuous centrifuges (disc bowl and decanter types) process a steady feed stream without interruption and are suited for high-throughput, steady-state operations. IPP stocks both batch centrifuges (basket and inverting filter types) and continuous centrifuges (disc bowl and decanter types) to match your process mode.
