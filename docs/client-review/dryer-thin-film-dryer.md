## Thin Film Dryers vs Thin Film Evaporators

A thin film dryer and a thin film evaporator share a rotor that spreads the feed on a heated wall, yet the feed each one takes and the product that leaves distinguish them. The table lines up the dryer against the evaporator.

| Factor | Dryer | Evaporator |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Feed | Viscous pastes, concentrated slurries and wet cakes | Pumpable liquids, dilute solutions and light suspensions |
| Output | A dry powder or solid, discharged at the end of the shell | A liquid concentrate plus a vapor distillate |
| Film Behavior | Solids stop flowing as the volatiles leave, and the rotor moves them toward the discharge | Liquid flows as a continuous film along the heated wall |
| Rotor Role | Spreads, mixes and conveys the drying product | Spreads and agitates the liquid film |

**Q: How does a horizontal thin film dryer work?**
A: A horizontal thin film dryer pumps the wet feed into a jacketed cylindrical shell, where a fast-turning rotor spreads it into a thin, turbulent film on the heated wall. Heat passes through the wall by conduction, the material moves from slurry through a paste phase to a dry granular solid on its way to the outlet, and the vapor leaves through a top port for condensing. Suppliers also sell the design as an agitated or wiped film unit.

**Q: What do the rotor blades in a thin film dryer do?**
A: In an agitated thin film dryer, the rotor blades spread the feed into a thin, turbulent film on the heated wall, and a bow wave in front of each blade edge intensifies heat and mass transfer. Hinged or pendulum blades swing outward with centrifugal force to follow changing product thickness, while fixed-clearance blades hold a set gap to the wall.

**Q: Can a thin film dryer cool a product as well?**
A: Yes. The same contact design also melts, cools, heats and mixes, and IMS lists IPP's Luwa SMS DK600 units as thin film continuous contact coolers with jackets rated 8 bar (116 psi) at 50 °C (122 °F).
