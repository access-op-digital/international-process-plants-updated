**Q: What is a wiped film evaporator and when is it used?**
A: A wiped film evaporator (also called thin film evaporator) uses mechanical wipers or blades to spread liquid feed into a thin film across a heated cylindrical surface. This creates very short residence times and gentle evaporation, making these units ideal for viscous, heat-sensitive, and fouling-prone materials. IPP stocks wiped/thin film evaporators from manufacturers including Luwa SMS, Pfaudler, LCI, and SMS Buss.

**Q: What is a rising/falling film evaporator?**
A: Rising and falling film evaporators use vertical tube bundles where liquid feed flows by gravity (falling film) or is carried upward by rising vapor (rising film). These evaporators are designed for high-throughput, continuous concentration of low-viscosity liquids with short contact times. IPP stocks rising/falling film evaporators from manufacturers including Wiegand, Buflovak/Blaw Knox, PMS, and APV.

**Q: What is a crystallizer/evaporator?**
A: Crystallizer/evaporators combine the functions of evaporation and crystallization into a single system. They concentrate solutions beyond the saturation point to precipitate dissolved solids as crystals. These systems are used for salt recovery, zero liquid discharge (ZLD), and chemical purification. IPP stocks crystallizer/evaporator units from manufacturers including Alfa Laval, Betchel, Gouda, and Pfaudler.

**Q: What is the difference between falling film and wiped film evaporators?**
A: Falling film evaporators rely on gravity to create a thin film inside vertical tubes, making them suited for high-volume, low-viscosity liquids. Wiped film evaporators use mechanical wipers to physically distribute the liquid across the heated surface, making them better suited for viscous, fouling-prone, or heat-sensitive materials where controlled film thickness and short residence time are critical.
