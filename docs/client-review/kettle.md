## Steam Jacketed Kettles vs Jacketed Reactors

Kettles and jacketed reactors both heat a batch through a jacket; vessel rating, closure and scale set them apart. Each row below places a kettle beside a jacketed reactor.

| Factor | Steam Jacketed Kettle | Jacketed Reactor |
|-----|-----|-----|
| Vessel | A bowl with a hinged or open top | A closed vessel with bolted or welded heads and nozzles |
| Pressure | Jacket rated for low-pressure steam, the vessel near atmospheric | Vessel and jacket both pressure rated |
| Discharge | Bottom outlet, or tilting and dumping the bowl | Bottom outlet valve |
| Agitation | Top-mounted anchor, paddle or sweep mixers | Sealed agitators, often with baffles |

**Q: How does a steam jacketed kettle work?**
A: A steam jacketed kettle surrounds the lower part of its bowl with a sealed jacket, and steam in the jacket condenses on the outer wall, releasing heat that passes through the metal into the product. Heat reaches the batch over a wide surface, and the jacket pressure sets the wall temperature. On mixer kettles, an agitator turns the batch so it heats evenly.

**Q: What agitators do the kettles carry?**
A: The Groen TA25SP carries a double agitator: an anchor plus four sets of two pitched paddles, driven by a US Varidrive at 716 to 2,150 rpm. The Groen TDC/2-20 uses a wide sweep steam kettle agitator with Teflon wipers and six blades on a Sterling kettle mixer with a manual lift.

**Q: What sizes and jacket ratings are available?**
A: The Groen TA25SP holds 94.6 L (25 gal) and the TDC/2-20 holds 18.9 L (5 gal), and each jacket is rated 3.1 bar (45 psi) at 148.9 °C (300 °F). A data sheet for either kettle follows on request.

**Q: What are the kettles made of?**
A: Each kettle is stainless steel 304, and the TA25SP adds a stainless steel 304 jacket, a hemispherical welded bottom and a flat top two-thirds hinged.
