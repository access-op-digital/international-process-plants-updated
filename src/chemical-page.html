From your brief to your site

How buying from IPP works

Bring your engineering requirements. IPP helps you establish the asset and service scope.

  1. Share your requirements

    Send the process, required capacity, materials of construction, operating conditions, destination and target timing. Include any stock numbers you have selected.

  2. Review specifications and inspect

    Request available photographs, drawings and equipment records. Arrange a video or on-site inspection with the IPP team.

  3. Agree the purchase scope

    Confirm the equipment included, commercial terms and services required. Ask about financing, which IPP advertises for its plants and equipment.

  4. Plan dismantling and relocation

    Coordinate teardown, loading and relocation requirements for the selected plant or system. Establish responsibilities and schedules before removal begins.

  5. Arrange shipping and start-up support

    IPP offers international shipping options, or buyers can arrange their own shipping. Discuss destination-site installation and start-up support as part of the project scope.

Services vary by asset and agreed scope. More about buying from IPP.

Put your assets back to work

Sell chemical plants and surplus equipment to IPP

IPP buys complete chemical plants, process lines and individual surplus equipment from shutdown, idled and restructured facilities. Its team handles intact, mothballed and partially dismantled plant sites.

IPP reports that Fortune 100 and Fortune 500 companies typically represent 95% of its sellers. Selling used chemical equipment to IPP replaces piecemeal disposal and ends the seller’s storage and maintenance costs.

Sell a plant ↗Sell equipment ↗

  1. Send your asset information

    Send the asset list, photographs, plant location, available technical documents and removal deadline. IPP’s forms to sell a plant or sell equipment collect each item.

  2. IPP values the assets

    IPP reviews the information and inspects the plant site where needed. IPP then values the assets for an outright purchase or for marketing to its global buyer network.

  3. Agree the sale structure

    IPP buys equipment as-is, where-is, as a principal buyer rather than a broker. A complete plant sale can include the land, buildings, equipment and intellectual property.

  4. Decommissioning, dismantling and removal

    IPP coordinates plant decommissioning, dismantling, packaging, freight and export. IPP’s team works with the owner’s EHS team on permit closure, emissions compliance and utility shutoffs.

Seller terms vary by asset and site. Sources: Sell to IPP, Sell plants and Sell equipment.

Chemical Process Plants Resources

Used chemical process plants and equipment from IPP

International Process Plants (IPP) buys and sells used process plants and equipment from its headquarters at 410 Princeton Hightstown Road, Princeton Junction, New Jersey.

IPP operates offices in 15 countries: Brazil, Canada, China, the Czech Republic, France, Germany, India, Italy, Mexico, Pakistan, Portugal, Romania, Turkey, the United Kingdom and the United States. IPP has served 160,000+ customers worldwide.

IPP’s chemical inventory covers complete plants, process lines and individual equipment such as reactors, heat exchangers, centrifuges, dryers, filters, distillation columns and tanks. IPP sources these assets from multinational chemical producers, toll manufacturers and decommissioned or restructured facilities.

The IPP group includes Gale Process Solutions (GPS), which fabricates new stainless steel process equipment, and Universal Glasteel Equipment (UGE), which supplies and re-glasses glass-lined equipment.

IPP affiliates acquired the GEO Specialty Chemicals site in Allentown, Pennsylvania, and agreed to acquire Novartis UK’s Grimsby API manufacturing site. In May 2024, IPP announced an agreement with BASF to market ammonia, methanol and melamine plants in Ludwigshafen, Germany.

Ross Gale, IPP’s vice president, wrote “Cost Engineering with Previously Owned Process Equipment” for Chemical Engineering in December 2024.
