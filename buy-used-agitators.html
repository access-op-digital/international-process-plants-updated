Frequently Asked Questions About Buying Used Agitators

Answers to the most common questions from engineers and procurement teams evaluating used agitator equipment.

IPP sells three main categories of agitators: glass-lined drive agitators designed for glass-lined reactors, standard drive agitators for open tanks and non-glass-lined vessels, and shaft/mixer agitators consisting of shafts with impellers and blades. Manufacturers include Pfaudler, Lightnin, DeDietrich, Philadelphia, Chemineer, Kestermann, Ekato, and others. Power ratings range from 0.2 to 100.7 kW (0.3 to 135 HP) with belt-drive and direct-coupled transmission options in right-angle, clamp-on, bridge-mount, and vertical configurations.
Glass-lined drive agitators are designed specifically for glass-lined reactors. They feature borosilicate glass-coated wetted parts — including the stuffing box, bearing housing, and shaft sealing area — to prevent metal contamination in corrosive pharmaceutical and chemical processes. Standard drive agitators use bare metal construction (carbon steel, stainless steel, or Hastelloy) and are suited for open tanks, stainless steel reactors, and other non-glass-lined vessels where corrosion resistance comes from the shaft and impeller metallurgy rather than a glass lining. Glass-lined drives from Pfaudler and DeDietrich are the most commonly sourced replacement agitators in the process equipment market.
IPP stocks agitators with power ratings from 0.2 kW (0.3 HP) for small pilot-scale reactors up to 100.7 kW (135 HP) for large production-scale mixing vessels. The most commonly stocked power ratings are in the 0.75 to 37.3 kW (1 to 50 HP) range, covering reactors from 75.7 L (20 gal) to 10,000 L (2,650 gal). Transmission types include belt drive and direct coupled. IPP's engineers can help match the correct power rating and impeller configuration to your reactor size and process viscosity requirements.
Matching an agitator to your reactor requires specifying the reactor manufacturer and model (e.g., Pfaudler 4RW, DeDietrich), vessel size and nozzle configuration, required power rating based on process viscosity and mixing intensity, shaft length and impeller type, and seal type (mechanical seal or stuffing box). IPP's on-staff process engineers assist with this matching process at no charge. For glass-lined reactors, the agitator drive must be specifically compatible with the reactor cover and nozzle geometry. Provide your reactor nameplate data and IPP will identify the correct replacement drive from inventory.
Chemical, pharmaceutical, food, beverage, cosmetics, and water treatment industries all rely on agitator drives for consistent mixing. In chemical manufacturing, agitator drives power mixers for homogenization, emulsification, and reaction support. In pharmaceutical production, they drive sanitary mixers for sterile blending and suspension. In food and beverage processing, agitator drives mix sauces, beverages, slurries, and syrups. In water and wastewater treatment, they agitate tanks for chemical dosing, flocculation, and biological treatment. In cosmetic and personal care manufacturing, agitator drives blend creams, lotions, and emulsions.
Agitator drives power the mechanical mixing elements; they can be sold separately for integration into existing tanks or as part of full mixer systems. A complete mixer assembly includes the drive, shaft, impeller, seal, and mounting hardware. IPP stocks both standalone drives and complete assemblies. Buying a standalone drive is the right choice when replacing a failed drive on an existing reactor where the shaft and impeller are still serviceable.
Routine inspection of seals, bearings, and motor components, along with lubrication and alignment checks, ensures reliable performance and extended drive life. For glass-lined drives, periodic spark testing of the glass lining detects early degradation before it causes process contamination. Belt-drive units require belt tension checks and periodic belt replacement. Direct-coupled units require coupling alignment verification.

Belt-drive and direct-coupled transmissions serve different applications. Belt-drive agitators (the majority of glass-lined drives in IPP stock) use a V-belt and pulley system between the motor and gearbox, providing speed flexibility and vibration isolation that protects the glass lining. Direct-coupled agitators connect the motor directly to the gearbox through a coupling, delivering higher mechanical efficiency and requiring less maintenance. Glass-lined reactor applications typically use belt-drive transmission because the belt absorbs vibration that could damage the glass lining. Standard drive applications in open tanks and stainless steel vessels more commonly use direct-coupled transmission for maximum power transfer.
