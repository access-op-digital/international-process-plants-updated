Answers to the most common questions from engineers and procurement teams evaluating used agitator equipment.

IPP sells three main categories of agitators: glass-lined drive agitators (233 units) designed for glass-lined reactors, standard drive agitators (64 units) for open tanks and non-glass-lined vessels, and shaft/mixer agitators (15 units) consisting of shafts with impellers and blades. Power ratings range from 0.2 to 100.7 kW (0.3 to 135 HP) with belt-drive and direct-coupled transmission options in right-angle, clamp-on, bridge-mount, and vertical configurations.
Glass-lined drive agitators are designed specifically for glass-lined reactors. They feature borosilicate glass-coated wetted parts — including the stuffing box, bearing housing, and shaft sealing area — to prevent metal contamination in corrosive pharmaceutical and chemical processes. Standard drive agitators use bare metal construction (carbon steel, stainless steel, or Hastelloy) and are suited for open tanks, stainless steel reactors, and other non-glass-lined vessels where corrosion resistance comes from the shaft and impeller metallurgy rather than a glass lining. Glass-lined drives from Pfaudler and DeDietrich are the most commonly sourced replacement agitators in the process equipment market.
IPP stocks agitators with power ratings from 0.2 kW (0.3 HP) for small pilot-scale reactors up to 100.7 kW (135 HP) for large production-scale mixing vessels. The most commonly stocked power ratings are in the 0.75 to 37.3 kW (1 to 50 HP) range, covering reactors from 75.7 L (20 gal) to 10,000 L (2,650 gal). Transmission types include belt drive (106 units) and direct coupled (24 units). IPP's engineers can help match the correct power rating and impeller configuration to your reactor size and process viscosity requirements.
Matching an agitator to your reactor requires specifying the reactor manufacturer and model (e.g., Pfaudler 4RW, DeDietrich), vessel size and nozzle configuration, required power rating based on process viscosity and mixing intensity, shaft length and impeller type, and seal type (mechanical seal or stuffing box). IPP's on-staff process engineers assist with this matching process at no charge. For glass-lined reactors, the agitator drive must be specifically compatible with the reactor cover and nozzle geometry. Provide your reactor nameplate data and IPP will identify the correct replacement drive from inventory.
Yes. IPP stocks 100 re-glassed agitator drives. The choice between Pfaudler and DeDietrich glass-lined drives depends primarily on your reactor manufacturer. Belt-drive and direct-coupled transmissions serve different applications. Belt-drive agitators (the majority of glass-lined drives in IPP stock) use a V-belt and pulley system between the motor and gearbox, providing speed flexibility and vibration isolation that protects the glass lining. Direct-coupled agitators (24 units in IPP stock) connect the motor directly to the gearbox through a coupling, delivering higher mechanical efficiency and requiring less maintenance. Glass-lined reactor applications typically use belt-drive transmission because the belt absorbs vibration that could damage the glass lining. Standard drive applications in open tanks and stainless steel vessels more commonly use direct-coupled transmission for maximum power transfer.
