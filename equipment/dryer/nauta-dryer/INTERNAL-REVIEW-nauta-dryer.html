Conical Screw vs Double Cone Dryers

Conical screw and double cone dryers both dry a batch against heated vessel walls, usually under vacuum, and they differ in what moves: the screw or the whole vessel. These factors separate the two designs.

Factor Conical Screw (Nauta) Dryer Double Cone Dryer
Vessel A stationary, vertical, jacketed cone A double-cone vessel that rotates on a horizontal axis
Agitation A cantilevered screw rotates on its own axis and orbits the cone wall The whole vessel turns to tumble the batch
Internal Parts Screw, shaft and seals in the product zone No internal moving parts
Batch Fill Works across a wide partial-batch range, since the screw does the mixing Needs a set fill level so the product cascades
Heating Jacket, plus a heated screw on some models Heated vessel walls

Available Materials of Construction

Conical dryers are specified by vessel material, and IPP's inventory covers the grades below.

Stainless Steel 316 & 316L

Stainless steel 316 and 316L vessels represent the largest share of IPP's conical dryer inventory, two of them with clad construction. Grades 316 and 316L contain molybdenum, which improves pitting resistance compared with grade 304.

Stainless Steel 304 & Austenitic

Stainless steel 304, austenitic and polished stainless steel vessels come on the Bolz Apparatebau MF250, JH Day, Krauss Maffei TMT 20/300 and Hosokawa Alpine units. Grades 304 and 316 both belong to the austenitic stainless steel family.

Hastelloy C-276

Hastelloy C-276 comes on the Summix Holland DF-300-VD3, with a coil jacket and orbital screw agitation. Hastelloy C-276 is a nickel-molybdenum-chromium alloy that resists both oxidizing and reducing media.

A Nauta conical screw dryer combines orbital mixing with indirect heating, usually under vacuum. A free-hanging screw rotates on its own axis to lift the product from the bottom of the cone, while an orbiting arm carries the screw along the cone wall. Steam, hot water or thermal oil in the jacket heats the product through the wall, the vacuum lowers the boiling point of the moisture or solvent, and the vapors leave through a filter at the top of the conical screw vacuum dryer on their way to a condenser.
The Nauta name comes from Johannes Ewardus Nauta, who founded Nautamix in Haarlem, the Netherlands, and introduced the original conical screw mixer in 1939. Hosokawa Micron acquired Nautamix and Vrieco and merged them into Hosokawa Micron B.V. in 1987, which builds the Nauta and Vrieco Nauta lines. Krauss Maffei, Bolz and other OEMs build conical screw dryers on the same principle, and IPP lists every Nauta dryer and conical screw dryer together, while the Nauta mixer has its own Nauta mixer page.
