Industrial chillers remove heat from liquids or gases through refrigeration cycles or heat exchange systems, ensuring equipment and processes operate within required temperature ranges. Refrigerants across the inventory include R-22, R-134a, R-410a, R-404a, R-123, and NH3 (ammonia). Materials of construction include carbon steel and galvanized. Condition options include good used, unused surplus, and new.

In chemical processing, chillers maintain reaction temperatures, cool reactors, and stabilize sensitive formulations. In food and beverage production, chillers cool ingredients, packaging lines, and production environments. In plastics processing, chillers remove heat from molds, extruders, and injection molding machines. Buying used chillers from IPP can save up to 50% of capital and 90% of lead time versus buying new. ⬅

Frequently Asked Questions About Buying Used Chillers

Answers to the most common questions from process teams and procurement teams evaluating used industrial chillers.

IPP stocks 36 industrial chillers across 6 subtypes: compressor chillers (14 units), water cooled chillers (8 units), low temp chillers (5 units), air cooled chillers (5 units), tower chillers (3 units), and ice machine chillers (1 unit). Browse the IMS inventory for current availability, or contact IPP to discuss your specific cooling requirements.
Air cooled chillers reject heat directly to the atmosphere through finned-tube condensers and condenser fans, making them suitable for facilities without cooling water availability. Water cooled chillers reject heat to a separate cooling water loop, typically through a cooling tower, and generally achieve higher efficiency and larger capacities. In IPP's inventory, water cooled chillers reach 3,000 refrigeration tons (10,550 kW), while air cooled chillers reach 113 refrigeration tons (397 kW).
Refrigerants across IPP's chiller inventory include R-22, R-134a, R-410a, R-404a, R-123, and NH3 (ammonia). Older units may use HCFC refrigerants such as R-22; HFC refrigerants such as R-134a and R-410a are common in more recent units; ammonia is used in industrial refrigeration applications. Note that R-22 has been phased out of new equipment production globally and may require refrigerant replacement or retrofit for long-term operation.
