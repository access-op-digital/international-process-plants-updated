Used Drive Agitator Questions & Answers

Common questions from process engineers and procurement specialists buying used drive agitators.

Selecting the correct HP depends on vessel volume, fluid viscosity, specific gravity, and mixing intensity. A 1,000-gallon low-viscosity blending application typically requires 3–5 HP, while a 5,000-gallon high-viscosity suspension may need 25–50 HP. IPP's engineers review your process parameters and recommend the appropriate drive from our 64-unit inventory.
Right-angle drives mount on the vessel nozzle or flange and transmit power through a 90-degree gearbox to the vertical shaft. They suit closed vessels with top nozzle access. Bridge-mount drives sit on a structural beam spanning the vessel opening, supporting the motor independently of the vessel shell. Bridge-mount configurations handle higher HP loads and serve large open-top tanks where the vessel wall cannot support the drive weight.
Clamp-on drives mount directly to the vessel rim without permanent installation, making them suited for multi-tank operations, batch changeovers, and temporary mixing needs. Flange-mounted drives bolt permanently to the vessel nozzle and provide stable, vibration-resistant mounting for continuous process operations. If you move the agitator between vessels, choose clamp-on. If the agitator stays on one vessel, choose flange-mount.
IPP's drive agitator inventory covers gear ratios from 2.5:1 to 70:1. Lower ratios (2.5:1 to 10:1) deliver higher output shaft speeds for low-viscosity blending. Higher ratios (25:1 to 70:1) produce lower output speeds with greater torque for high-viscosity suspension and heavy-duty chemical processing. IPP provides the gear ratio specification for every drive in stock.
IPP's drive agitators produce output shaft speeds from 25 to 184 RPM. Low-speed drives (25–45 RPM) handle high-viscosity mixing and suspension. Mid-speed drives (58–120 RPM) serve general chemical blending and reaction mixing. Higher-speed drives (120–184 RPM) suit low-viscosity blending and dispersion applications.

Drive agitators use bare metal construction (carbon steel, stainless steel, or Hastelloy) and serve open tanks and non-glass-lined vessels where corrosion resistance comes from the shaft and impeller metallurgy. Glass-lined drive agitators feature borosilicate glass-coated wetted parts specifically designed for glass-lined reactors where metal contamination must be prevented. If your vessel is glass-lined, you need a glass-lined drive. If it is not, a standard drive agitator is the correct choice.
A drive agitator is the complete drive assembly — motor, gearbox, and mounting hardware that provides the mechanical power to rotate the agitator shaft. A shaft/mixer agitator is the shaft and impeller assembly that connects to the drive and does the actual mixing inside the vessel. If your drive motor and gearbox have failed, you need a drive agitator. If your shaft is corroded, bent, or the impeller is damaged but the drive still works, you need a shaft/mixer replacement.
