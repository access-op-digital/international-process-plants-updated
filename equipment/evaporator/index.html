Many evaporators in IPP's inventory are vacuum capable. Vacuum operation is used to protect heat-sensitive materials by lowering the boiling point during evaporation. Browse the inventory or contact IPP to identify vacuum-rated units.
A wiped film evaporator (also called thin film evaporator) uses mechanical wipers or blades to spread liquid feed into a thin film across a heated cylindrical surface. This creates very short residence times and gentle evaporation, making these units ideal for viscous, heat-sensitive, and fouling-prone materials. IPP stocks wiped/thin film evaporators from manufacturers including Luwa SMS, Pfaudler, LCI, and SMS Buss.
Rising and falling film evaporators use vertical tube bundles where liquid feed flows by gravity (falling film) or is carried upward by rising vapor (rising film). These evaporators are designed for high-throughput, continuous concentration of low-viscosity liquids with short contact times. Crystallizer/evaporators combine the functions of evaporation and crystallization into a single system. They concentrate solutions beyond the saturation point to precipitate dissolved solids as crystals. These systems are used for salt recovery, zero liquid discharge (ZLD), and chemical purification. IPP stocks crystallizer/evaporator units from manufacturers including Alfa Laval, Betchel, Gouda, and Pfaudler.
Falling film evaporators rely on gravity to create a thin film inside vertical tubes, making them suited for high-volume, low-viscosity liquids. Wiped film evaporators use mechanical wipers to physically distribute the liquid across the heated surface, making them better suited for viscous, fouling-prone, or heat-sensitive materials where controlled film thickness and short residence time are critical.
