### Block 2

Used mills are essential in industries where reducing solid materials to fine powders or controlled particle sizes is critical. Whether you’re grinding active pharmaceutical ingredients, pulverizing pigments, or micronizing mineral fillers, mills offer the precision and flexibility needed for modern process demands. With more than 47 years of experience , IPP provides used, surplus and secondhand milling systems from 20 global plant sites and maintains a ready-to-ship inventory of 15,000+ systems . 
 IPP stocks a wide range of industrial mills, including hammer mills, jet mills, ball mills, colloid mills, attrition mills, and roller mills from leading brands like Hosokawa, Fitzpatrick, Netzsch, Pallmann and WAB. 
 Applications and industries 
 
 In pharmaceuticals , for micronizing APIs and excipients to improve bioavailability 
 In food and beverage , for grinding spices, sugars, grains and dairy ingredients 
 In chemicals and pigments , for dispersing solids into coatings, inks and additives 
 In mineral processing , for dry or wet milling of calcium carbonate, kaolin and metal ores 
 
 Types of materials processed 
 
 Dry solids such as powders, resins, pigments and minerals 
 Wet slurries requiring emulsification or particle dispersion 
 Heat-sensitive materials needing low-impact, cryogenic or inert atmosphere milling 
 
 Common materials of construction 
 Mills must be built to handle abrasive, corrosive or sanitary materials: 
 
 304 or 316L stainless steel for food, pharma and hygienic applications 
 Hardened carbon steel or tool steel for heavy-duty mineral and metal grinding 
 Ceramic-lined or jacketed designs for corrosive or temperature-controlled processes 
 
 Key features of industrial mills 
 
 Fine and ultrafine particle size capability (down to sub-micron levels) 
 Configurable for batch or continuous operation 
 Explosion-proof and dust containment options for safety 
 Variable-speed controls and screen size flexibility 
 CIP-ready and cGMP-compliant models available
