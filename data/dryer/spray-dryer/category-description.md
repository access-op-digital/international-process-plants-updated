Spray dryers
convert liquids into dry powders by atomizing the feed into a hot air stream, allowing for fast, controlled evaporation. Ideal for heat-sensitive products, spray dryers are used in a wide range of industries to ensure consistent particle size, solubility and purity. With over
47 years of experience
,
IPP
supplies surplus, secondhand and used spray dryers from
20 complete process plant sites
and an unmatched inventory of more than
15,000 ready-to-ship systems
.
Spray dryers can be configured for co-current, counter-current or mixed-flow operation and often feature cyclones, bag filters or scrubbers for product recovery and emission control.
Primary uses across industries
In
pharmaceuticals and biotech
, they dry active ingredients, enzymes, and injectables
In
food and dairy
, they process milk powders, proteins, flavors and instant beverages
In
chemical manufacturing
, they dry pigments, polymers, detergents and fine resins
In
ceramics and materials science
, they produce powders for advanced manufacturing
Types of materials processed
Liquid slurries and emulsions
Heat-sensitive solutions requiring fast drying
Products requiring controlled particle size and powder consistency

Dryer-Spray FAQ
Have questions about Spray Dryers or our services? Learn more about our products and
why customers choose IPP.
What types of products are ideal for spray drying?
Liquids, slurries and emulsions that need to be converted to fine powders, such as milk, APIs, pigments and proteins.
How is a spray dryer different from other dryers?
Spray dryers atomize the feed and expose it to hot air instantly, making them faster and more suitable for liquids than rotary or tray dryers.
What are key maintenance areas for spray dryers?
Inspect atomizers, heat exchangers, exhaust filters and product recovery systems regularly for optimal performance and cleanliness.
