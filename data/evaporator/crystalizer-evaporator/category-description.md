CS, with rubber lining, Vapor Body, with Alloy support accessories, forced circulation crystallizer, part of a Zero Discharge System (ZLD). The system's major equipment consists of a shell and tube evaporator heater, Vapor body, recirculation pump and centrifuge. Rated for 50 GPM flow10% moisture) and consumes 20 psi steam at 25,00 lbs./hr. Following is a short process description: The feed from Brine Evaporator enters the recirculation piping above the recirculation pump. The feed is mixed with recirculating brine slurry and pumped through the heater where it increases in temperature and is sent to the vapor body. The hot brine flashes down to the saturation pressure inside the vessel, producing vapor and the cycle is repeated. Crystals are formed continuously in crystallizer vapor body and have to be removed. Concentrated product is removed from the system, passed through a centrifuge to remove the solid crystals and is returned to the evaporator recirculation line. The crystals are sent to a landfill. Vapor from the crystallizer is sent to a small barometric condenser using cooling water to condense the vapors. Condensate is mixed with incoming cooling water and is discharged back to the cooling tower. Vent from the condenser is vented by a CS ejector, through a silencer, and out to the atmosphere. Prime condensate from the condensed plant stream is sent back to the primary feed heater to assist in heating incoming Brine Evaporator feed. The following are main components of the system: Stock #228100- crystallizer heater is 5,700 Ft2 of surface area, with 1040 straight tubes, 1.25”OD x 0.028” wall thickness x 12 Feet long, and are constructed of Titanium. The tube sheets are SS316 Ti clad. Heads are made of SS alloy AL6XN & Shell of SS316L. The Crystallizer stock # 228156 is 16’ Dia. x 30’ Overall Height with 60” cone bottom. It is made of rubber lined Carbon steel. (the crystallizer vessel was removed , we have a drawing so a new vapor body can be constructed.) The Crystallizer recirculation pump, stock #228104 is 19,000 GPM with 150 HP motor and fabricated from SS alloy. The centrifuge stock #228101 is Bird Bowl type, 24" diameter x 38" long, rated for 18,304 Lbs./hr. with SS316L contact parts, 30 HP Motor with SS alloy product contact parts.

Used evaporators
are thermal separation systems that concentrate solutions, recover solvents, or reduce waste volume by vaporizing liquid components. These systems are essential across industries where product purity, yield, and cost-efficiency matter. With more than

Evaporators are available in multiple configurations—falling film, rising film, forced circulation, wiped film and multiple-effect systems—each tailored for specific viscosity, temperature sensitivity and throughput needs.
Primary uses across industries
In
pharmaceuticals
, evaporators concentrate APIs and remove solvents during intermediate processing
In
chemical manufacturing
, they purify products and reclaim valuable solvents from process streams
In
food and beverage
, they concentrate juices, dairy products, extracts and syrups
In
wastewater treatment
, they reduce volume and recover reusable components
Types of materials processed
Heat-sensitive or solvent-based mixtures
Aqueous solutions, organic solvents and slurries
Viscous liquids and high-solid content mixtures

What’s the difference between falling film and forced circulation evaporators?
Falling film evaporators are ideal for heat-sensitive materials and lower viscosities, while forced circulation systems handle higher solids and fouling-prone feeds.
Can used evaporators be modified for new applications?
Yes – many units can be reconfigured with new instrumentation, controls or materials of construction to meet changing process needs.
Are multi-effect evaporators more energy efficient?
Absolutely – they reuse vapor from one stage to heat the next, reducing overall steam consumption and operational costs.
